The building

England's last Hansa.

Hanse House on the South Quay is the only complete Hanseatic League warehouse left standing in England — a working merchant's compound granted to Baltic traders in 1475.

The Hansa was a confederation of Baltic and North German merchant guilds — Lübeck, Hamburg, Gdańsk, Riga — who handled the medieval trade between the North Sea and the Baltic. For more than two hundred years their cogs and hulks tied up at this Quay, unloading fur, pitch, timber and grain, and loading up English wool and cloth in return.

The building changed hands in 1751, was extended in the Georgian style, and has since been a customs house, council offices, and a wedding venue. We occupy a single small room of it.

The tearoom

Small, slow, properly made.

We're not chasing a chain — we're a few tables, a kitchen out the back, and the time to bake before opening.

Scones are pulled from the oven in the morning. Cakes go on the counter once they've cooled. The afternoon tea menu changes a little with the season — what's in, what looks good, whether the strawberries are worth bothering with.

Loose-leaf tea, by the pot, with a proper strainer. Coffee unhurried. Mismatched china because matching feels wrong inside a 15th-century warehouse.
