A Hanseatic address

Tea inside a 15th-century merchant's warehouse.

Hanse House is the only complete surviving Hanseatic League warehouse in England — a quiet stone-and-timber building looking out over the Great Ouse, where Baltic merchants once unloaded grain, fur and timber.

We've made a small room of it. Mismatched china, big windows on to the Quay, scones still warm from the oven, and a pot of properly-brewed loose-leaf tea on the table while you watch the tide turn. No bookings required, though Saturdays fill up.

What we do

Three things, done properly.

There's no all-day kitchen pretending to be everything. We do three things, and we do them with care.

  • Afternoon tea

    Sweet or savoury. A tiered stand of finger sandwiches, warm scones, clotted cream and the day's cake — with a bottomless pot.

    By the table

  • Homemade cakes & scones

    Baked on site each morning. Victoria sponge, lemon drizzle, fruit and plain scones, gluten-free options.

    From the counter

  • Loose-leaf teas & coffee

    A small, considered tea list — proper black, fragrant green, herbal infusions — and unhurried coffee.

    By the pot
